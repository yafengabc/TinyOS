What changed and how it was proved
----------------------------------
1. RP2040 RDID.  The RP2040 has no QMI; the flash hangs off a PL022 SSI.
   rp_flash_rdid() (section .data.flash_ram, i.e. SRAM) copies the boot2
   image out of XIP, calls rom_connect/rom_exit_xip, then drives the SSI by
   hand: SSIENR=0, CTRLR0=7 (DSS=7 for 8 bits, Motorola SPI, mode 0),
   SER=1 (CS0), SSIENR=1, five byte exchanges through DR0 with SR.TFNF and
   SR.RFNE polling, SSIENR=0, SER=0, flush, then the boot2 copy re-enters
   XIP.  Same shape as the SDK's flash_do_cmd() on this chip.  Result bytes
   rx[1..3] are combined into the 24-bit ID and stored through the out
   pointer; the capacity decode uses decimal 20..24 bounds.

2. plat_flash_detected_bytes() caches.  The first caller is ramdisk_init(),
   which kmain() runs before cpu_init() and before interrupts are enabled -
   so the probe runs single-core with nothing to park, and the SMP image
   needs no parking handshake at boot.  Later callers (/proc/flash, df) read
   the cache.

3. plat_block_count() follows plat_lfs_blocks() now (was PLAT_LFS_BLOCKS * 8),
   so the measured size actually reaches the mounted filesystem.

4. Why min() was wrong.  min(detected, configured) protects the case where
   board.conf overstates the chip, but it also makes a deliberately
   conservative board.conf a hard ceiling.  Reading the part is strictly
   better information: 4 MiB measured on a 1 MiB configuration means the
   chip really is 4 MiB.  The mount guard in ramdisk_init still compares a
   superblock against this value, so an oversized image is refused.

5. Disassembly check (rpi-pico): rp_flash_rdid is at 0x20000020 in the SRAM
   section, 496 bytes; the SSI register writes, the SR bit polls, the
   three-byte combine (`lsls`/`adds`) and the store through `out` are all
   present, followed by `blx` to the boot2 copy.  No decode folding - the
   capacity bounds are decimal.
